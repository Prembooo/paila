# Better Questions & Good Sources

## 1. Learning Goal (सिकाइ लक्ष्य)

By the end, you can ask a clear, useful question.
You can pick a source you can trust.

## 2. Let's Learn (सिकौं)

A good question gets you a good answer.
A vague question gets a vague answer.

Make your question clear and small.
Say what you want, and add one helpful detail.

For example, instead of "tell me about farming",
ask "how do I grow tomatoes in a small home garden?"

This works for a search engine and for AI too.
**AI literacy:** a clear question helps AI give a useful answer.

Then check the source of any answer.
Ask: who made this, and do other good sources agree?

A trustworthy source is clear about who wrote it.
It matches what other careful sources say.

## 3. Look and Name (हेरौं र नाम भनौं)

| Weak question | Better question |
|---|---|
| "Tell me about football." | "What are the basic rules of football?" |
| "Help with farming." | "How do I grow tomatoes at home?" |
| "Explain computers." | "What does the CPU in a computer do?" |

## 4. Practice (अभ्यास)

Do this with your facilitator near.

1. Take a vague question and make it clearer.
2. Add one helpful detail to it.
3. Ask: who would be a trustworthy source?
4. Say how you would check the answer.

**Success:** you improved a question and named a good source.

## 6. Activity (क्रियाकलाप)

Pick a topic you like — for example football, a song, farming, or your village. Any topic is fine.
Write one clear question about your topic, and name one source you would trust.
For example: "What drills improve passing? I would trust a coaching video and a sports site."
There is no single right answer — your choice is what matters.
