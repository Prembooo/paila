# Passwords & Accounts

## 1. Learning Goal (सिकाइ लक्ष्य)

By the end, you can make a **strong password**.
You can name two ways to keep an account safe.

## 2. Let's Learn (सिकौं)

An **account** is your own space in an app or website.
A **password** is the secret code that opens it.

A weak password is short and easy to guess.
A strong password is longer and mixes different things.

![A weak password is short; a strong one is longer and mixed.](figures/password-strength.svg)

A good password is **long** — 12 characters or more.
Joining a few words with a number and a symbol works well.
Make it easy for you, but hard for others.

Two more habits keep your account safe:
**Turn on two-step verification**, and **do not reuse one password** everywhere.

A **passkey** is a newer, safe way to sign in.
You unlock with your fingerprint or face — nothing to type or steal.

## 3. Look and Name (हेरौं र नाम भनौं)

| Habit | Why it helps |
|---|---|
| Long (12+), mixed | Hard for others to guess. |
| Two-step verification | A second check stops most break-ins. |
| One password per account | One leak cannot open the rest. |
| Passkey (fingerprint/face) | Nothing to type, nothing to steal. |

## 4. Practice (अभ्यास)

Do this with a sample password — never a real one.

1. Build a sample password with 12 or more characters.
2. Add a number and a symbol to it.
3. Check it against the rule above.
4. Name two habits you would turn on.

**Success:** your sample meets the rule and you name two habits.

## 6. Activity (क्रियाकलाप)

Pick a topic you like — for example football, a song, farming, or your village. Any topic is fine.
Imagine an account for your topic. Write one sample strong password (not a real one).
Then list two habits you would turn on to keep it safe.
For example: "Password: GoalKick#9. I turn on two-step verification."
There is no single right answer — your choice is what matters.
