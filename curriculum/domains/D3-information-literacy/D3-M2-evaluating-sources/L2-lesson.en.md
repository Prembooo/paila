# Evidence and Cross-Checking

## 1. Learning Goal (सिकाइ लक्ष्य)

By the end, you can ask **what evidence** a source gives.
You can cross-check a claim against two sources.

## 2. Let's Learn (सिकौं)

Last lesson we asked who, when, and why.
Now we add the fourth question.

**What evidence is there?**
Evidence means proof, like numbers, photos, or names.
A good source shows where its facts came from.
A weak source just says "trust me".

But one source is not always enough.
So we do a **cross-check**.
This means we look for the same fact in another place.

Try to find the fact in **two independent sources**.
"Independent" means not all from the same person or place.
Two pages by the same writer are not independent.

![Check a claim against two independent sources.](figures/verify-two-sources.svg)

If two independent sources agree, you can trust it more.
If they disagree, the claim is still unverified.

## 3. Look and Name (हेरौं र नाम भनौं)

Here are the four trust questions together.

| Question | What to look for |
|---|---|
| **Who** made it? | A clear name or group. |
| **When** was it made? | A recent date. |
| **Why** was it made? | To help, not only to sell. |
| **What evidence?** | Numbers, photos, or named proof. |

## 4. Practice (अभ्यास)

Do this with your facilitator near.

1. Pick one claim, like "this fruit helps health".
2. Find the fact in one source. Note the name.
3. Find the same fact in a second, different source.
4. Check: are the two sources independent?

**Success:** you checked one claim against two independent sources.

## 6. Activity (क्रियाकलाप)

Pick a topic you like — for example football, a song, farming, or your village. Any topic is fine.
Write one claim about your topic.
Then name two independent sources you could use to check it.
For example: "A coach and a sports book are two independent sources for a football rule."
There is no single right answer — your choice is what matters.
